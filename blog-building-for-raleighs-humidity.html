Building for Raleigh's Humidity

Raleigh sits in a mixed-humid climate, with long, sticky summers and cool winters. Homes here have to handle moisture in both directions, and that shapes nearly every building decision.

← All articles3 min read

Humidity affects comfort, energy use and indoor air quality. When it's managed poorly, it also leads to condensation, musty odors and mold. Building in the Triangle means designing for summer humidity first.

Vapor control that fits the climate

In cold northern climates, walls get a vapor barrier on the inside. In a mixed-humid climate like ours, moisture pushes inward in summer and outward in winter, so walls need to be able to dry. That often means smart vapor retarders or vapor-open assemblies rather than plastic sheeting, which can trap moisture against framing. The right assembly depends on the cladding, insulation and sheathing, so it should be designed as a system.

Right-size the air conditioning

An oversized AC system cools the house quickly and shuts off before it removes much moisture, leaving the home cool but clammy. A properly sized system, based on a load calculation rather than a rule of thumb, runs longer cycles and dehumidifies better. Variable-speed equipment helps further.

Add dedicated dehumidification

Efficient, airtight homes need less cooling, which can mean the AC runs too little to control humidity, especially in spring and fall. A whole-home dehumidifier tied into the HVAC system keeps relative humidity in the healthy 30–50% range year-round.

Bring in fresh air carefully

Mechanical ventilation is essential in a tight house, but in our climate, outdoor air brings humidity with it. An energy recovery ventilator (ERV) exchanges stale air for fresh while transferring some of the moisture back out, reducing the load on your AC and dehumidifier.

Deal with crawlspaces and basements

Many older Raleigh homes have vented crawlspaces, which can pull humid summer air under the house where it condenses on cool surfaces. Sealed or conditioned crawlspaces with proper drainage and a vapor barrier on the ground are often a healthier approach. This is one of the most impactful upgrades in a remodel.

Manage water outside

Gutters, downspout extensions and grading that slopes away from the foundation keep bulk water out. Heavy summer storms are common here, so these details matter.
