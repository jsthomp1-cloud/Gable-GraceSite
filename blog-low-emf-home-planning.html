Low-EMF Home Planning

Some homeowners want to reduce electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure where they sleep and spend the most time. The most effective steps are simple, and easiest to plan before walls are closed.

← All articles3 min read

Electromagnetic fields come from wiring, appliances and wireless devices. The science on everyday household levels is still debated, and we don't make medical claims about it. Many of our clients simply prefer to reduce exposure where it's easy to do, especially in bedrooms. Here's how we plan for that.

Hardwire the house

Running Ethernet cable to offices, TVs, desks and bedrooms lets you rely less on Wi-Fi and gives you faster, more reliable internet. It's inexpensive to install during construction and costly to add later.

Control your Wi-Fi

A Wi-Fi kill switch, a wall switch or timer that powers down the router, lets you turn wireless off overnight. Placing the router centrally but away from bedrooms, and using wired access points instead of mesh repeaters near sleeping areas, also helps.

Plan the wiring layout

Keep the electrical panel, meters and major appliances away from bedroom walls where possible. Avoid running heavy circuits along the wall behind a headboard. Thoughtful wiring practices can reduce magnetic fields from household wiring, and a qualified electrician can verify readings after installation.

Bedroom choices

Some clients add a switch that cuts power to bedroom circuits at night. Others simply keep electronics out of the bedroom and charge phones elsewhere. Both are easy to design for.

Lighting

Choose quality LED fixtures and dimmers with low flicker. Warm, dimmable lighting in the evening supports a calmer wind-down regardless of where you stand on EMF.

Measure, don't guess

If reducing EMF is a priority, consider testing with a qualified consultant before and after construction. Measuring tells you which steps actually made a difference in your home.
