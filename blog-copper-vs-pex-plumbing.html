Copper vs. PEX Plumbing

PEX has become the default supply line in new homes. Copper is the long-standing alternative. Both can be good choices. Here's how to decide.

← All articles3 min read

Your water supply lines carry everything you drink and cook with, so it's reasonable to care what they're made of. Most new homes now use PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) because it's fast to install and less expensive. Copper costs more but has a long track record. The right choice depends on your priorities and your water.

The case for PEX

PEX is flexible, resists freeze bursts better than rigid pipe, needs fewer fittings, and installs quickly, which lowers labor cost. It doesn't corrode or build up scale the way metal pipe can. For many homes, it's a sound, code-approved choice.

The concern with PEX

Like many plastics, new PEX can release small amounts of organic compounds into water, which some studies have detected as taste and odor, particularly in the first weeks. Levels typically decline as the pipe is flushed and used. Products certified to NSF/ANSI 61 meet drinking-water safety standards. PEX also shouldn't be exposed to sunlight before installation, since UV degrades it.

The case for copper

Copper has been used in homes for generations, is recyclable, and doesn't release plastic compounds. It's naturally resistant to bacterial growth and tolerates heat well. For clients who want to minimize plastic in their drinking water, copper is the traditional answer.

The concern with copper

Copper costs significantly more in both materials and labor. It's also sensitive to water chemistry. Acidic or aggressive water can corrode copper over time, causing pinhole leaks and elevated copper levels at the tap. That's why a water test should come before the decision, particularly for homes on wells.

A middle path

Some clients choose copper for the main lines and drinking-water fixtures and PEX elsewhere. Others pair PEX with point-of-use filtration at the kitchen sink. Whole-home filtration can also address disinfectants and sediment regardless of pipe type.

Our approach

We walk clients through both options with their water test results and budget in hand. The goal is a clear, informed decision, not a default.
