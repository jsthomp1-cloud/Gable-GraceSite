Low-VOC and Natural Finishes

That new-house smell is mostly chemicals off-gassing from paints, adhesives and finishes. With the right products, a new home can smell like wood and plaster instead.

← All articles3 min read

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) are carbon-based chemicals that evaporate at room temperature. Many building products release them, most heavily in the first weeks and months after installation. Some VOCs are irritants, and some are associated with longer-term health concerns. Choosing better finishes is one of the most direct ways to improve indoor air in a new home or remodel.

Understanding the labels

Low-VOC and zero-VOC refer to regulated VOC content in the product, usually the base paint. Tinting can add VOCs back, so ask about the colorant system too. GREENGUARD Gold is a third-party certification based on actual emissions testing, which tells you more than content claims alone. Look for it on paint, flooring, cabinetry and furniture.

Paint

Most major paint lines now offer zero-VOC options that perform well. Mineral paints and limewash are natural alternatives that create a soft, matte finish with real depth. They suit the warm, timeless look many of our clients want.

Plaster

Lime and clay plasters are naturally breathable and help buffer indoor humidity. Lime is also alkaline, which discourages mold growth on the surface. They take skilled application and cost more than painted drywall, so many clients use them in feature rooms, primary suites and bathrooms.

Wood finishes

Hardened natural oil finishes, such as those based on linseed or tung oil, penetrate the wood and can be spot-repaired. Water-based polyurethanes have much lower VOCs than older solvent-based products. Whatever the finish, allow full cure time with good ventilation before moving in.

Adhesives, caulks and cabinetry

These are easy to overlook. Specify low-VOC adhesives and sealants, and ask what cabinet boxes are made of. Some composite panels use formaldehyde-based binders. Solid wood or panels certified for ultra-low formaldehyde emissions are better choices.

Timing matters

Even good products off-gas a little. Schedule finishes so they have time to cure, ventilate well during and after application, and flush the house with fresh air before move-in.
