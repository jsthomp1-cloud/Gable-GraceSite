Whole-Home Water Filtration

The right water filtration system depends on your water. Start with a test, then build the system around what the results show.

← All articles3 min read

Filtration is one of the most requested healthy-home upgrades, and one of the most oversold. A good system is designed around a water test, sized for your household's flow, and maintained on schedule. A system that skips those steps is often money spent without much benefit.

Step one: test your water

If you're on city water, read your utility's annual water quality report and consider an independent lab test at your tap, since water can change between the treatment plant and your faucet. If you're on a well, which is common in the communities around Raleigh, test regularly with a certified lab. The results tell you what's worth filtering.

Whole-home (point-of-entry) systems

These treat all water entering the house, so showers, laundry and every tap benefit. A typical setup includes:

  • Sediment filtration to catch sand and particles and protect the rest of the system.
  • Carbon filtration to reduce chlorine, chloramine (with catalytic carbon), and many organic compounds that affect taste and odor.
  • Softening or scale control if hardness is high, to protect fixtures and appliances.
  • Specialty treatment such as iron removal or UV disinfection, typically for wells.

Point-of-use systems

For drinking and cooking water, reverse osmosis (RO) at the kitchen sink removes a broad range of dissolved contaminants. RO also removes minerals, and some clients add a remineralization stage for taste. Many homes combine whole-home carbon with RO at the kitchen.

Plan the space

Whole-home systems need room near where water enters the house, a drain for backflushing, and easy access for filter changes. Planning a dedicated spot in the garage or mechanical room avoids awkward retrofits.

Maintenance is part of the design

Every filter has a service schedule. An expired carbon filter stops working and can harbor bacteria. Choose a system with filters you can easily get and change, and put reminders on the calendar.
