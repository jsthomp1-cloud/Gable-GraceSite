Preventing Mold During Construction

Many mold problems in new homes are built in before anyone moves in. The fix is discipline on the job site: keep materials dry, measure, and don't close walls on wet framing.

← All articles3 min read

Mold needs three things: moisture, a food source and time. Wood framing, drywall paper and dust supply the food. The one variable a builder can control is moisture, and construction is when a house is most exposed to it.

Protect materials before they're installed

Lumber, sheathing, insulation and drywall should be stored off the ground and covered. Drywall in particular should never be installed if it has been rained on. Delivering materials closer to when they're needed reduces how long they sit exposed.

Dry in quickly

The faster a house gets a roof, weather barrier, windows and doors, the less rain the framing absorbs. Scheduling around the dry-in milestone is one of the most effective mold-prevention steps there is.

Measure before closing walls

Framing lumber is considered "dry" at about 19% moisture content or lower, and many building scientists recommend getting it lower still before insulation and drywall go in. A moisture meter takes the guesswork out. We check framing in multiple locations and wait when readings are high, even if it pushes the schedule.

Manage humidity inside the house

Drywall mud, paint, concrete and plaster all release water as they cure. Running dehumidifiers and ventilating during finishes keeps that moisture from settling into framing and insulation. Using propane or kerosene heaters without ventilation adds moisture too, since combustion produces water vapor.

Detail the envelope correctly

Window and door flashing, a continuous drainage plane, proper roof-to-wall details, and grading that moves water away from the foundation all keep water out for the life of the home. These details are invisible once siding goes on, which is why we document them with photos.

Start clean

Before move-in, a thorough cleaning and new HVAC filters remove construction dust. Running the HVAC during dusty work without filter protection can spread debris through the ductwork, so ducts should be sealed or protected until finishes are complete.
