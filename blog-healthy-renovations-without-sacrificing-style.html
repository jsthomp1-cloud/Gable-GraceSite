Healthy Renovations Without Sacrificing Style

A healthier home and a beautiful one aren't competing goals. Many of the most timeless materials are also the healthiest. The key is knowing where to focus.

← All articles3 min read

Renovation is the best chance to improve an existing home's health, because walls, floors and systems are opened up. It's also when you're most exposed to dust and new materials. A thoughtful plan handles both.

Choose materials that age well

Natural stone, solid hardwood, unlacquered brass, lime plaster, zellige and terracotta tile, and linen are both beautiful and low in chemical emissions. These are the materials that give a home warmth and character, and they tend to look better with age rather than dated.

Fix what's behind the walls

When walls are open, look for signs of past moisture, update old wiring, add insulation and air sealing, and plan for ventilation. In older homes, it's also the time to address crawlspace moisture and consider plumbing upgrades. These improvements are invisible but have the biggest effect on how the house feels to live in.

Test before you disturb

Homes built before 1978 may contain lead paint, and older homes can contain asbestos in flooring, insulation or textured ceilings. Testing before demolition protects your family and the crew, and federal rules require lead-safe practices for many renovation projects in pre-1978 homes.

Control dust during construction

Sealing work areas with plastic barriers, using negative air pressure, protecting HVAC returns and cleaning with HEPA vacuums keep construction dust out of the rest of the house. If you're living at home during the project, ask how dust will be contained.

Spend where it matters most

If budget is a factor, prioritize the bedroom and the kitchen. You spend the most hours in bedrooms, and kitchens combine water, cooking pollutants and many finishes. Good range-hood ventilation vented outdoors, low-VOC cabinetry and filtered water at the sink are high-impact upgrades.

Finish thoughtfully

Choose low- and zero-VOC paints, natural oil or water-based wood finishes, and GREENGUARD Gold certified products. Allow time for finishes to cure before moving back into the space, and air the house out well.
